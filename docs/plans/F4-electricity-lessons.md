# F4 — Electricity Lessons

## Purpose

The electricity lessons teach students the main concepts they need to understand before and while using the CircuitLore circuit lab.

Lessons should be short, visual, interactive, and written for students around ages 10–14.

The goal is not to give students large amounts of text to memorize. Each lesson should explain a concept, show it visually, let the student interact with it, and connect it to something they already know.

---

## Lesson Structure

Most lessons should follow a similar pattern:

1. Introduce the concept.
2. Connect it to history when appropriate.
3. Explain the concept simply.
4. Show a visual example.
5. Let the student interact with the idea.
6. Connect it to everyday life.
7. Ask a short question or challenge.
8. Save completion.
9. Continue the learning journey.

---

## Lesson 1 — Electrical Safety

Students should learn basic electrical safety before using the rest of CircuitLore.

Topics may include:

- Electricity can be dangerous.
- Water and electricity should be kept apart.
- Electrical outlets should not be played with.
- Damaged electrical cords can be dangerous.
- Real electrical systems should only be worked on with proper knowledge and supervision.
- CircuitLore experiments are virtual and should not encourage unsafe real-world experiments.

### Goal

Students understand that electricity is useful but needs to be treated safely.

---

## Lesson 2 — Electrical Charge

Introduce the idea that matter contains electrical charges.

Topics may include:

- Positive and negative charge
- Attraction and repulsion
- Static electricity
- Simple examples students recognize

### Possible Interaction

Students could interact with objects to see examples of attraction or repulsion.

### Goal

Students understand that electrical effects begin with electric charge.

---

## Lesson 3 — Current

Introduce electrical current as the movement of electric charge.

Topics may include:

- What current means
- Why a complete path is needed
- What happens when the path is broken
- Simple comparison to movement through a path

### Possible Interaction

Show charge moving through a complete circuit and stopping when the circuit is opened.

### Goal

Students understand that current needs a complete path.

---

## Lesson 4 — Voltage

Introduce voltage as the electrical push that helps move charge through a circuit.

Topics may include:

- What voltage represents
- Batteries as a source of voltage
- Different amounts of electrical push

### Possible Interaction

Allow the student to compare simple visual examples using different voltage levels.

### Goal

Students understand that voltage helps drive current through a circuit.

---

## Lesson 5 — Conductors and Insulators

Students learn that different materials behave differently around electricity.

### Conductors

Examples may include:

- Copper
- Aluminum
- Other metals

### Insulators

Examples may include:

- Rubber
- Plastic
- Glass

### Possible Interaction

Students choose different materials and predict whether they will allow a simple virtual circuit to work.

### Goal

Students can identify the basic difference between a conductor and an insulator.

---

## Lesson 6 — Resistance

Introduce resistance as something that limits the flow of current.

Topics may include:

- What resistance does
- Resistors
- Why resistance is useful
- Simple effects of changing resistance

### Possible Interaction

Students adjust resistance and observe a visual change in a virtual circuit.

### Goal

Students understand that resistance affects electrical current.

---

## Lesson 7 — Electrical Components

Before entering the circuit lab, students should recognize the basic components they will use.

Initial components:

- Battery
- Wire
- Light bulb
- Switch
- Resistor

For each component, students should learn:

- What it is
- What it does
- What its visual representation looks like in CircuitLore

### Goal

Students recognize the basic components used in the circuit lab.

---

## Lesson 8 — Open and Closed Circuits

Students learn the difference between an open and closed circuit.

### Closed Circuit

A complete path allows current to flow.

### Open Circuit

A broken path prevents current from flowing.

### Possible Interaction

Students open and close a switch and watch what happens to a virtual light bulb.

### Goal

Students can explain why a bulb turns on in a closed circuit and turns off when the path is broken.

---

## Lesson 9 — Series Circuits

Introduce circuits where components share a single path.

Topics may include:

- One electrical path
- Multiple components
- What happens if the path is broken

### Possible Interaction

Students build or modify a simple series circuit.

### Goal

Students recognize the basic structure of a series circuit.

---

## Lesson 10 — Parallel Circuits

Introduce circuits that contain multiple paths.

Topics may include:

- Multiple paths
- Difference from a series circuit
- Simple real-world connections

### Possible Interaction

Students compare a simple series circuit with a simple parallel circuit.

### Goal

Students recognize the basic difference between series and parallel circuits.

This lesson may be simplified if the circuit simulation becomes too complex for the project scope.

---

## Lesson 11 — Electricity Today

The final learning section connects the concepts students have learned to modern life.

Possible examples:

- Homes
- Lighting
- Appliances
- Phones
- Computers
- Transportation
- Power systems
- Renewable energy

This lesson should also connect back to the historical portion of CircuitLore.

### Goal

Students understand that modern electrical technology developed from discoveries and concepts they learned throughout the journey.

---

The goal is to create a flow such as:

---

## Lesson Content

Each lesson may contain:

- Short text
- Character dialogue
- Images
- Animation
- Diagrams
- Interactive demonstrations
- Questions
- Mini challenges
- Modern examples

Lessons should avoid long blocks of text.

---

## Lesson Navigation

Students should be able to:

- Continue to the next section
- Return to appropriate previous content
- Revisit completed lessons
- See whether a lesson is complete
- Return to the Journey Map

Some lessons may remain locked until required previous content has been completed.

---

## Lesson Completion

A lesson should have a clear completion point.

Completion may require:

- Viewing the required lesson sections
- Completing an interaction
- Answering a required question
- Completing a short challenge

The exact requirement can vary depending on the lesson.

---

## Saved Progress

When a student completes a lesson, CircuitLore should save the completion to their account.

Possible information:

- Student ID
- Lesson ID
- Completion status
- Completion date
- Attempts where appropriate

Detailed progress storage will be handled in F6.

---

## Content Accuracy

Electricity lessons should use reliable educational sources.

AI may help:

- Organize content
- Simplify wording
- Brainstorm activities
- Assist development

AI should not be treated as the source of truth for scientific information.

Important scientific information should be checked before being published in CircuitLore.

---

## Age-Appropriate Content

Lessons should be understandable for students around ages 10–14.

Content should:

- Use clear language
- Explain unfamiliar words
- Use examples students recognize
- Avoid unnecessary technical detail
- Introduce concepts gradually
- Use visuals where possible

The goal is to simplify the explanation without teaching incorrect information.

---

## Accessibility

Lessons should consider:

- Readable text
- Alternative text for important images
- Keyboard accessibility
- Reduced-motion support
- Clear instructions
- Information that does not depend only on color
- Text alternatives for important animation or audio

---

## Acceptance Criteria

F4 will be considered complete when:

1. CircuitLore has a defined set of core electricity lessons.
2. Lessons follow a consistent basic structure.
3. Content is appropriate for the target age group.
4. Lessons connect to historical content where appropriate.
5. Lessons include visual or interactive learning where appropriate.
6. Students can navigate through lessons.
7. Students can revisit completed lessons.
8. Lesson completion can be recorded.
9. Lessons connect to the larger F2 Learning Journey.
10. Lessons prepare students to use the virtual circuit lab.
11. Scientific information is based on reliable sources.
12. Electrical safety is introduced before hands-on circuit concepts.

---

## Testing

Test:

- Opening a lesson
- Moving through lesson content
- Completing an interaction
- Completing a lesson
- Saving lesson completion
- Returning to a completed lesson
- Locked lesson behavior
- Navigation to the next lesson
- Different screen sizes
- Keyboard navigation
- Reduced-motion behavior

Lesson content should also be reviewed for:

- Scientific accuracy
- Readability
- Age appropriateness
- Clear instructions

The lessons should be understandable, interactive where appropriate, connected to the historical journey, and able to record student completion.
