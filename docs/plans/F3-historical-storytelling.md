# F3 — Historical Storytelling

## Purpose

The historical storytelling feature introduces students to electricity by showing how people learned about it over time.

Instead of beginning with definitions, CircuitLore will introduce important people, discoveries, and experiments and then connect those discoveries to the electrical concepts students will learn later.

The goal is to make the history feel like part of the experience rather than a textbook chapter.

---

## Main Experience

Historical sections should follow a simple pattern:

1. Enter a historical scene.
2. Meet a historical figure.
3. Learn what question or problem they were exploring.
4. See or interact with the discovery or experiment.
5. Learn what the experiment taught us.
6. Connect that discovery to electricity today.
7. Complete a short interaction or challenge.
8. Continue to the next part of the journey.

---

## Historical Figures

The final list of historical figures will be determined through research.

Possible figures may include people connected to major developments in our understanding of electricity.

Each person included should have a clear purpose in the learning journey.

CircuitLore should not include historical figures simply because they are famous.

Each figure should help introduce or explain an electrical concept that the student will use later.

---

## Historical Research

Historical information must be researched before it is added to the application.

For each historical figure or event, I should document:

- Name
- Time period
- Important discovery or contribution
- Experiment or work connected to electricity
- What the discovery taught us
- How it connects to a modern electrical concept
- Sources used

AI may help organize or simplify information, but it should not be treated as the historical source.

---

## Historical Scene

Each historical section should have a visual environment connected to the person or discovery.

Possible environments include:

- Laboratory
- Workshop
- Study
- Outdoor experiment
- Early electrical demonstration

The scene should use CircuitLore's realistic-cartoon visual style.

The goal is to make the environment feel historical while still being welcoming to younger students.

---

## Character Introduction

When a historical figure appears, the student should quickly understand:

- Who they are
- When they lived
- What they were interested in
- Why they matter to this lesson

Introductions should be short.

CircuitLore should avoid large paragraphs of historical information.

---

## Character Dialogue

Historical figures may guide the student through short dialogue.

Dialogue should:

- Be understandable for ages 10–14
- Use short sections
- Focus on the lesson
- Avoid unnecessary information
- Allow the student to continue at their own pace

Characters should not claim to say exact historical quotes unless the quote has been verified.

When dialogue is created for storytelling purposes, it should be treated as educational narration rather than a real historical quotation.

---

## Historical Experiment

When possible, a historical section should include an interactive or visual demonstration.

For example, a student may:

- Select an object
- Make a prediction
- Move something
- Turn something on or off
- Choose what they think will happen
- Observe an animation
- Answer a question about the result

The interaction does not need to perfectly recreate the original experiment.

Its purpose is to help the student understand the idea the experiment demonstrated.

---

## "What Did We Learn?"

After the historical discovery, CircuitLore should clearly explain what the discovery taught us.

This should act as the bridge between history and science.

Example structure:

**What happened?**

A short explanation of the experiment or discovery.

**What did we learn?**

The electrical idea demonstrated by it.

**Why does it matter today?**

An example of how that idea connects to modern electricity or technology.

---

## Modern Connection

Each major historical section should connect to something students recognize today.

Possible examples:

- Light switches
- Batteries
- Electrical outlets
- Phones
- Appliances
- Power lines
- Electronics

This helps students understand that the history is connected to the technology around them.

---

## Student Interaction

Students should not spend the entire historical experience clicking "Next."

Historical sections should include small interactions where appropriate.

Examples:

- Make a prediction
- Choose an object
- Match a discovery to an idea
- Put events in order
- Identify a conductor or insulator
- Complete a simple experiment
- Answer a short question

More detailed quizzes will be handled by F5.

---

## Animation

Historical figures may:

- Enter or leave a scene
- Point toward an object
- React to an experiment
- Introduce an item
- Change expression
- Guide attention toward part of the screen

Objects may also animate during demonstrations.

Animation should support what is being taught.

Students should not be required to sit through long animations before continuing.

---

## Realistic-Cartoon Style

Historical figures should be inspired by their real appearance when reliable visual references exist.

The goal is not photorealism.

Characters should look:

- Recognizable
- Friendly
- Expressive
- Age-appropriate
- Consistent with the rest of CircuitLore

Historical clothing, environments, and equipment should also be reasonably accurate while fitting the visual style.

---

## Historical Timeline

The student should be able to understand that discoveries happened at different points in history.

A simple timeline may show:

- Historical figure
- Approximate date or period
- Discovery
- Completed/unlocked status

The timeline may also become part of the larger Learning Journey from F2.

---

## Saving Progress

CircuitLore should save when a student completes a historical section.

Possible saved information:

- Historical section completed
- Activity completed
- Date completed
- Next section unlocked

Detailed progress storage will be handled in F6.

---

## Accessibility

Historical storytelling should not depend entirely on animation.

Important information should also be available through text or another accessible method.

The feature should consider:

- Captions/text for spoken content
- Alternative text
- Keyboard navigation
- Readable text
- Reduced motion
- Controls for moving through dialogue

---

## Acceptance Criteria

F3 will be considered complete when:

1. Historical content is organized into individual sections.
2. Each major section introduces a historical figure or discovery.
3. Historical information is based on researched sources.
4. Students receive short, age-appropriate explanations.
5. Historical discoveries connect to an electrical concept.
6. Historical discoveries connect to modern electricity where appropriate.
7. At least some sections contain student interaction.
8. Historical scenes use the CircuitLore visual direction.
9. Animations support the lesson rather than block it.
10. Students can move through dialogue at their own pace.
11. Completion can be recorded for student progress.
12. Historical sections fit into the F2 Learning Journey.

---

## Testing

Test:

- Entering a historical section
- Character introduction
- Moving through dialogue
- Interactive experiment
- Incorrect interaction
- Correct interaction
- Completing a historical section
- Progress saving
- Returning to a completed section
- Reduced-motion behavior
- Keyboard navigation
- Different screen sizes

Content should also be reviewed for historical accuracy and readability.

---

## Dependencies

F3 depends on:

- F1 Authentication and User Profiles
- F2 Learning Journey
- F6 Student Progress
- F11 Animation and Visual Experience

It will also connect closely with F4 Electricity Lessons.

---

## Out of Scope for F3

F3 will not handle:

- Full circuit simulation
- Account management
- Detailed quiz systems
- Parent/teacher reports
- AI tutoring
- Overall progress calculations

Those belong to other features.

---

## Definition of Done

F3 is done when students can move through a working historical learning experience where researched historical figures and discoveries help teach electrical concepts.

The student should be able to enter a scene, learn about a discovery, interact with the lesson, understand what the discovery taught us, see how it connects to modern electricity, and continue to the next part of CircuitLore.
